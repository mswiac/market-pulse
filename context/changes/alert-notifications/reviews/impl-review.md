## Findings

### F1 — Uniform 10% re-arm margin can shrink to near-zero at low thresholds

- **Severity**: ℹ️ OBSERVATION
- **Dimension**: Architecture
- **Location**: src/worker/lib/alert-evaluation.ts:80
- **Detail**: `RE_ARM_MARGIN_FRACTION` (10%) applies identically to PRICE and RSI thresholds. An RSI alert with a very low threshold (e.g. 5) gets a tiny margin, so it could re-fire on noise right at the line. This matches the tested and deliberately-chosen design (10% was picked and verified against research during planning) — a forward-looking edge case, not a bug.
- **Fix**: PRICE alerts keep a 10%-of-threshold margin; RSI alerts use a fixed 10-point margin instead (RSI is a bounded 0-100 index, not a monetary value, so a percentage doesn't scale sensibly).
- **Decision**: FIXED — `src/worker/lib/alert-evaluation.ts` now branches on `alert_type`; added `test/worker/alert-evaluation.test.ts` coverage for the fixed RSI margin (not-yet-retreated and re-arm cases). 98/98 tests pass, typecheck clean.

### F2 — Small TOCTOU window between computeArmed's read and the write batch

- **Severity**: ℹ️ OBSERVATION
- **Dimension**: Safety & Quality
- **Location**: src/worker/routes/alerts.ts:130
- **Detail**: `computeArmed()` reads `market_data` in a separate query from the later INSERT/UPDATE batch — if the cron wrote fresh `market_data` in between, the computed armed state could be a moment stale. Negligible for a single-user, daily-cron app with no meaningful request concurrency.
- **Decision**: SKIPPED — accepted risk at current scale (single user, once-daily cron, no real request concurrency).
